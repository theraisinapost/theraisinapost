Editorial approach

We choose stories because of their public consequences and the quality of evidence available, not because a claim is loud or newly viral. An announcement is a starting point for reporting, not proof of an outcome. A judgment has a legal scope; a budget number has a time period; a corporate dispute may have competing parties whose claims must be evaluated separately.

Start with the record

Wherever possible, our work begins with original materials: official releases and orders, legislative texts, court records, statistical publications, regulatory filings, budget statements, company disclosures or named institutional reports. We pair them with reputable, attributed reporting and link material claims to sources that readers can inspect. When a source cannot be independently checked, the limitation should be visible rather than hidden by confident language.

Separate what happened from what might happen

A consultation is not a law. A government target is not a completed result. A preliminary dataset is not the same as a revised final release. An allegation is not a finding. A minister's statement, an analyst's projection and a measured outcome are three different kinds of evidence; they should not be blended into one conclusion.

Numbers require units, dates and denominators. We seek useful comparison points and flag revisions or methodological changes that affect interpretation. Where two official accounts appear inconsistent, we explain the mismatch and the additional record needed to resolve it. We do not turn a gap in evidence into a claim of misconduct.

Make uncertainty readable

Analysis can explore implications, incentives and plausible consequences, but it is identified as analysis rather than treated as new reporting. When accounts disagree or evidence is insufficient, readers should be told what remains unknown. We do not invent interviews, quotations, eyewitness descriptions, access to unpublished documents or firsthand reporting. A document-led review is described for what it is.

The objective is a publication that makes complicated decisions more intelligible without pretending that complexity has disappeared. Read the original editorial policy.

Our sections

The website navigation follows the publication's current Substack menu. The Power Brief, Business and Governance are subjects and reading routes; they are not substitutes for the individual article's original context.

The Power Brief

Our source-linked morning digest covering consequential developments in government, the economy and the world. Reports distinguish decisions from proposals, forecasts from results and evidence from interpretation.

Business

Ownership, corporate governance, markets, financial decisions and industry. We use disclosures and original records to understand the interests and consequences behind a business story.

Governance

Public institutions, courts, legislation, regulation and state capacity. We clarify the authority of each institution, the relevant legal record and what can be concluded from it.

Corrections & feedback

A strong public record is one that can be corrected. If a consequential fact is wrong, the publication's policy is to correct the public article promptly. Substantive corrections should explain what changed and when; small typographical or formatting repairs may not require a notice. An article may also be updated when new evidence materially changes the account.

To report an error, provide the headline or article URL, the precise passage, the nature of the mistake and, when possible, an original document or credible source supporting the change. Send it to raisinapost@substack.com or reply to a newsletter. We review the supporting evidence before changing the story.

A good-faith disagreement with an interpretation is not necessarily a factual correction. We consider such challenges, explain uncertainties and distinguish new evidence from competing views. The original editorial policy is the reference for formal corrections practice.

Images, audio & AI

Readers should be able to tell the difference between a photograph and an editorial illustration. Conceptual imagery, including AI-generated artwork, is labelled as illustration rather than presented as a documentary record of a real event. Credits are provided where required, and photographs are used only where rights permit.

Synthetic narration in audio is disclosed. AI-assisted drafting, research organization or production does not remove editorial responsibility: claims and citations still require independent checking. We do not present generated imagery as evidence, invent interviews or treat model output as a verified source. The same standards apply whether an article is short, visual or long-form.

Independence & conflicts

The published policy states that The Raisina Post does not accept third-party advertising, paid placement or payment for favourable coverage. A relevant personal or commercial connection is to be disclosed. Affiliated messages are labelled and kept separate from reporting.

This includes a disclosed affiliated house message for Alle's ClinX, a brand of Champaran Innovatives Private Limited. Such a message is an affiliation disclosure, not an editorial source or direction for reporting. The existence of a relationship should not be concealed, and readers should be able to judge the work with that context available.

If policies change as the publication develops, material revisions should be dated on the canonical policy page.

Submissions & correspondence

We welcome verifiable story leads, corrections, public documents, informed challenges and thoughtful analysis relevant to our areas of coverage. A useful submission explains what happened, why it has public consequence, what the available evidence establishes and what questions remain unanswered.

Write to raisinapost@substack.com, or contact the publication through Substack. Clearly identify any conflicts or affiliations relevant to the material. Sending an idea does not guarantee coverage or publication. Do not transmit sensitive personal records or confidential material through an insecure channel.

Rights & attribution

Headlines and short summaries appear on this GitHub Pages site through the publication's public RSS feed. Full editions and their authoritative versions remain on Substack. This index is intended to help readers discover and navigate the work, not to imply that articles are freely licensed for reproduction.

Always credit the original publication and link back to the article. For republication of full text, commissioned illustrations, photographs or audio, contact the Editorial Desk to discuss permission. Reuse rights for third-party imagery and other material may differ from rights in the publication's own writing.
